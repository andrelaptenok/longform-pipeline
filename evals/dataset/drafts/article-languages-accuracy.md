# Three years, one language, no classroom

Everybody knows somebody who is learning Spanish since four years and still
cannot order a coffee. I was that person until I have changed how I studied
rather than how much.

What worked was uncomfortable and unglamorous. Speaking from the first week,
badly, to stranger online did more in month than a year of filling gaps in
workbook, because conversation punishes hesitation and workbook forgives it.
Watching a series with subtitles in the language which I was learning, never in
my own, turned an evening off into hour of listening. And ten minutes of
vocabulary every single morning beat two hours every Sunday, which I was
inevitably skipping and then feeling guilty about it for a week.

Motivation, though, is where most learners are failing, and it fades for
predictable reason: after first few months, progress stops to be visible. The
beginner who learns two hundred words feels transformed; the intermediate
learner who learns two hundred more feels exactly same as before. What rescued
me was measuring something different than my own sense of improvement. I kept a
note about every conversation which I survived, and looked back on it whenever I
felt stuck.

Nobody needs a classroom for learning a language. What everybody needs is a
reason to open the app on fourteenth consecutive evening.
