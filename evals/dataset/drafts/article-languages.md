# Three years, one language, no classroom

Everybody knows somebody who has been learning Spanish for four years and still
cannot order a coffee. I was that person until I changed how I studied rather
than how much.

What worked was uncomfortable and unglamorous. Speaking from the first week,
badly, to a stranger online did more in a month than a year of filling gaps in
a workbook, because a conversation punishes hesitation and a workbook forgives
it. Watching a series with subtitles in the language I was learning, never in my
own, turned an evening off into an hour of listening. And ten minutes of
vocabulary every single morning beat two hours every Sunday, which I would
inevitably skip and then feel guilty about for a week.

Motivation, though, is where most learners actually fail, and it fades for a
predictable reason: after the first few months, progress stops being visible.
The beginner who learns two hundred words feels transformed; the intermediate
learner who learns two hundred more feels exactly the same as before. What
rescued me was measuring something other than my own sense of improvement.
I kept a note of every conversation I survived, and looked back at it whenever
I felt stuck.

Nobody needs a classroom to learn a language. What everybody needs is a reason
to open the app on the fourteenth consecutive evening.
