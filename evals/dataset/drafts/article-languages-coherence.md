# Three years, one language, no classroom

The beginner who learns two hundred words feels transformed. Motivation is where
most learners actually fail. Nobody needs a classroom to learn a language. The
intermediate learner who learns two hundred more feels exactly the same as
before.

Speaking from the first week, badly, to a stranger online did more in a month
than a year of filling gaps in a workbook. Everybody knows somebody who has been
learning Spanish for four years and still cannot order a coffee. A conversation
punishes hesitation. A workbook forgives it. I was that person until I changed
how I studied rather than how much.

Ten minutes of vocabulary every single morning beat two hours every Sunday. I
would inevitably skip the two hours and then feel guilty about it for a week. I
kept a note of every conversation I survived. I looked back at it whenever I
felt stuck. Watching a series with subtitles in the language I was learning,
never in my own, turned an evening off into an hour of listening.

Progress stops being visible after the first few months. What everybody needs is
a reason to open the app on the fourteenth consecutive evening. Measuring
something other than my own sense of improvement rescued me. What worked was
uncomfortable and unglamorous.
